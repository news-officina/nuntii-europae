European Commission launches defence campaign as poll finds 71% back an EU army

The 9 November campaign, called "European defence: protect what matters", promotes joint investment, as a GLOBSEC survey finds wide, though not universal, backing for common defence.

14 November 2026

Correction: Corrected: an earlier version called the GLOBSEC survey the Commission's own polling and dated a rejection by EU officials to April.

The European Commission launched a communication campaign on 9 November 2026. It is called "European defence: protect what matters" and aims to show what joint cooperation and investment in security add for member states. A GLOBSEC poll found that 71 percent of Europeans want the EU to have its own army.

The Commission says the plan accelerates investment in collective security. Its Readiness 2030 plan, launched in 2025, mobilises up to €800 billion to close capability gaps and strengthen European industrial capacity. The Security Action for Europe instrument, known as SAFE, has so far disbursed almost €13.5 billion across 11 member states.

GLOBSEC's 2026 survey covered 14 countries, including 11 EU member states, Ukraine, the United Kingdom and the United States. It found 82 percent support in Romania, 78 percent in Lithuania and 75 percent in France. According to Politico, other senior EU officials, among them the EU's top diplomat Kaja Kallas, have rejected the idea of an EU army.

The Commission points to its own polling: in the latest Eurobarometer, 81 percent of Europeans back a common defence and security policy. HarrisX carried out GLOBSEC's survey, GLOBSEC Trends 2026: The Reality Check, between 11 and 19 May 2026, questioning 13,730 people across Europe and North America. It included 1,010 respondents in Bulgaria with a margin of error of plus or minus 3.1 percentage points. Andrius Kubilius, the European Commissioner for Defence and Space, wrote that Europeans want "more Europe" and "stronger Europe" inside NATO for the defence of Europe.

Guntram Wolff, a senior fellow at Bruegel, said in February that a sovereign European army was "quite unlikely, unless circumstances change dramatically". He argued that citizens would rather see their countries deliver on joint defence. GLOBSEC's survey found 83 percent of EU respondents favour Europe taking on more responsibility. It also found that only 49 percent in Europe believe European countries could ensure their own security without strong US support.
