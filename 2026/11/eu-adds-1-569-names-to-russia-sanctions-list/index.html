EU adds 1,569 names to Russia sanctions list in what it calls largest round yet

The new listings, adopted by member states on 12 October, bring the total to nearly 4,600 and target companies at the heart of Russia's missile production. More measures are still being prepared.

3 November 2026

The Council of the European Union adopted a new Russia sanctions package on 12 October 2026, adding 1,569 individuals and entities in a round the Council described as its largest to date. The listings bring the total to close to 4,600. Those named face an asset freeze, a ban on providing funds or economic resources to them and, for natural persons, a travel ban to the EU.

The legal acts are Council decision (CFSP) 2026/2279 and Council implementing regulation (EU) 2026/2281, both of 12 October 2026, amending the 2014 sanctions regime concerning Ukraine's territorial integrity. Kaja Kallas, the EU's High Representative, said the round was the largest since 2022 and that more than half of the listings concern the ballistic missile production chain. "This is not the time to ease pressure on Moscow but to increase it," she said.

The Council's breakdown of the 12 October 2026 list shows 826 Russian companies and 743 individuals serving as their chief executives, plus 77 people linked to the September elections to the State Duma, Russia's parliament, held in occupied Ukrainian territories. Those firms have a combined annual turnover of at least 20 billion euros and employ more than 250,000 people.

According to the Council, the listed companies supply key components for Russia's missiles and UAVs, unmanned aerial drones, as well as its aircraft, including semiconductors, communication equipment, aviation, satellite and missile systems, explosives, ammunition, and optics and navigation systems. The package adds no new sectoral, trade, energy or financial restrictions. EU ambassadors agreed on the listings on 7 October, after several weeks of deliberation and legal review, before ministers adopted them formally on 12 October. Work on a further sanctions package continues.

Following a request from Estonia, the Council removed a Russian supplier to an Estonian rare-earth processor and an associated individual from the proposed list. Estonia said it would reconsider the two listings in 2027. The European Parliament will debate 'Further EU sanctions against Russia in response to the escalating attacks against Ukraine: cutting of the sources financing the Russian war' on 12 November 2026.
