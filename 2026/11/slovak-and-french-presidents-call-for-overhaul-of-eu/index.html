Slovak and French presidents call for overhaul of EU electricity pricing

Peter Pellegrini and Emmanuel Macron met in Paris for their first bilateral talks, with a 120 million euro contract for three Airbus helicopters signed in the days around the meeting.

13 November 2026

Slovak President Peter Pellegrini and French President Emmanuel Macron called for a reform of European electricity price formation so that it no longer follows the price of natural gas. They met in Paris on 11 November 2026 for their first bilateral talks. The meeting produced a contract worth 120 million euros for three heavy Airbus helicopters for Slovak police and rescue services, according to the Slovak presidential office.

Pellegrini said the two leaders discussed which European leader could represent the European Union in negotiations on Ukraine, someone with a mandate whom all parties would respect, not technical-level observers. He said Europe cannot be left out of the negotiations or of the future security arrangement of Europe. A unified European position on ending the war matters, because in his words a military solution would bring no desired result.

Slovakia produces almost no electricity from gas, he said, which is why he considers it illogical that power prices follow the gas price. The reactor for the planned new nuclear source in Slovakia will be supplied by the American company Westinghouse, he said. The project still leaves room for French companies in components, nuclear fuel and other technologies, he said, and France could cooperate on the reuse of spent nuclear fuel. Macron, for his part, expressed interest in cooperation in nuclear energy.

Pellegrini said the contract for the three heavy helicopters from the French manufacturer was signed in the days around the meeting with Macron. He added that France will bid in a tender for lighter models. Pellegrini said France is Slovakia's eighth most important trading market, and he noted that French companies employ more than 35,000 people in Slovakia.

On the war itself, Pellegrini said a diplomatic solution must be sought as soon as possible. Only a diplomatic settlement, an end to the fighting and a search for security guarantees is a possible path, he said.
