Slovak security firm links new email espionage tool to Russian hacking group Turla

ESET says the tool, found on a compromised Exchange server at a governmental organisation in Romania, was set to flag NATO and EU email traffic. A second victim in Kazakhstan was also identified.

11 November 2026

The Slovak security company ESET has identified a new cyber-espionage toolset, named Cereal, which it attributes with high confidence to Turla, a hacking group aligned with Russia. ESET said the toolset, which runs on Microsoft Exchange servers and collects selected email, was found at a governmental organization in Romania, on a server compromised on 23 December 2025. There it was configured to flag email sent to or received from NATO and the EU.

ESET says it has identified two victims of Cereal: a governmental organization in Kazakhstan and one in Romania. The Romanian outlet Gandul reports that the identity of the targeted institution and the amount of information that may have been extracted have not been made public. The Dutch newspaper De Telegraaf reported that Russian hackers had followed NATO and EU emails and spoke of intercepted sensitive information, while the technology site Tech Insider notes that the available reporting confirms only that the software was set to search for NATO- and EU-related mail traffic, and that no captured messages have been shown nor any confirmed as secret or classified.

ESET's attribution rests on two grounds, the company says. Cereal was used at the same time as another component that security researchers have publicly linked to Turla, and it shares design ideas with older Turla malware. The toolset is built to be hard to find. ESET says it lacks many features normally used to identify malware, such as beacons sent back to command-and-control servers. Most of its work happens in the computer's memory rather than in permanent files.

Turla, also known as Snake, has been active since at least 2004 and is widely assessed to be part of Russia's Federal Security Service, according to ESET. The company says the group has used compromised Exchange servers as operational infrastructure before, and that the technique is rare, employed by only a handful of hacking groups. ESET describes Cereal as modular malware, built in .NET, that collects email and receives commands.

ESET announced its findings on 10 November 2026, about eleven months after the first infection the researchers found. The identity of the targeted Romanian institution, and how much information may have been taken, has still not been made public.
