Russia says no agreement for Europe to join Abu Dhabi talks with Ukraine and US

Grigory Karasin of the Federation Council dismissed France's plans to attend as wishful thinking on 9 November. Ursula von der Leyen says the European Union will still be represented alongside its partners.

9 November 2026

No agreement has been reached for European states to take part in the next Russia-US-Ukraine talks in Abu Dhabi on 10-11 November, Grigory Karasin, first deputy chairman of the international affairs committee of the Federation Council, Russia's upper house of parliament, said on 9 November. He called France's plans to attend wishful thinking. Ursula von der Leyen, the European Commission President, said on Monday the EU will be present alongside its partners.

Bjoern Seibert, Ursula von der Leyen's chief of staff, will represent the European Commission at the Abu Dhabi talks, according to Politico. Anna-Maria Boura, chief foreign policy adviser to António Costa, president of the European Council, the EU body of heads of state and government, will represent the Council at the talks, Politico reported. Donald Trump, meanwhile, said there was a very important meeting taking place, that he believes the war will end soon, and that the only reason it will end is because of him.

Jean-Noel Barrot, the French Foreign Minister, said in an interview on 8 November that the Europeans will be well-represented, and that France will be in Abu Dhabi. An EU official told Politico the European representatives would not be inside the room. Costa agreed on 6 November to put EU representation at the talks on the agenda of the informal EU leaders' meeting in Dublin on Friday.

The talks are formally trilateral: Russia and Ukraine at the table, with the United States acting as mediator, as Dmitry Peskov, the Kremlin's press secretary, told reporters last week. He said the Europeans have their own channels of communication with Washington. Peskov confirmed on 3 November that the Russian delegation, led by Vladimir Medinsky, an aide to President Vladimir Putin, will take part. Steve Witkoff, the US envoy, said the discussions will focus on post-war security arrangements, de-escalation, humanitarian efforts, and next steps toward peace.

Kaja Kallas, the EU's foreign policy chief, has warned that any agreement the EU is not party to, it will not implement either. Radosław Sikorski, the Polish Foreign Minister, called a deal made over Europe's head impossible and asked for a ceasefire before talks on European security arrangements. The foreign ministers of the Group of Seven, the forum of the leading industrialised democracies, gather in Paris on Monday and Tuesday, under France's presidency, days before the Abu Dhabi talks, with support for Ukraine high on the agenda.
