US agencies say North Korean hackers stole $6.3 billion from Binance

The exchange suspended withdrawals and froze about $3 billion of the stolen funds, while Pyongyang dismissed the accusations as absurd.

3 November 2026

The FBI and the US Secret Service have formally accused North Korean hackers of stealing more than 6.3 billion dollars from Binance, the world's biggest cryptocurrency exchange. The theft is the largest of its kind on record, according to the blockchain analysis firm Chainalysis, exceeding the 1.5 billion dollars taken from Bybit in February 2025.

Binance suspended withdrawals immediately after the theft as a precaution while it completed security checks. The exchange was shut down for several days while it used reserve funds to replace the stolen coins. Binance said it will cover all losses from the SAFU fund and the company's funds. Deposits and withdrawals resumed on Sunday morning. "User funds are safe," Binance said, adding that an updated proof of reserves will follow once the remediation is complete.

Binance said the attacker used a compromised DevOps engineer's laptop to access a service container and submit unauthorized withdrawal transactions. Working with blockchain partners, the exchange froze the affected assets, with about 3 billion dollars caught on-chain, the company said. In an update on October 30, it stated that deposits remain unaffected and continue to operate as normal.

Bleeping Computer reports that the stablecoin issuers Tether, Circle, Ripple and World Liberty Financial froze about 2.67 billion dollars tied to the theft. What remains outside any issuer's reach is larger in some ways: the attacker still holds more than 420,000 ETH and 18,270 BTC, roughly 2.4 billion dollars.

North Korea rejected the accusations in a statement carried by KCNA, its state news agency. Kim Yo-jong, the sister of the country's leader Kim Jong-un, called the FBI's claims absurd rhetoric bringing nothing new, referring to earlier United States accusations over the February 2025 Bybit theft. Binance said it is working with law enforcement and blockchain intelligence partners to trace and recover the remaining funds.
