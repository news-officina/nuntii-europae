US Treasury Secretary moves to military base after death threats linked to Iran

The United States has also moved refuelling aircraft from a British air base to Germany and other locations, and charged three men accused of plotting an attack on another British base on behalf of Iran's Revolutionary Guard.

12 November 2026

US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent has moved from his home in the Washington area to a military base after death threats, the New York Times reported. The threats were attributed to Iran, the paper said, citing three informed sources. A Treasury spokesperson said in an emailed statement to Reuters that the secretary remains committed to executing Operation Economic Outcast and maintaining the blockade, which together are collapsing Iran's economy.

The US military said on Tuesday that it had moved aircraft from an airbase in the United Kingdom. It did so in the wake of a threat from Iran, the Wall Street Journal reported, citing people familiar with the matter. Two refuelling tankers and several other tankers left RAF Mildenhall, a British air base in eastern England. They diverted to Germany and other locations on Monday.

On Wednesday, the United States charged three men suspected of plotting an attack on RAF Fairford, another British air base. They are accused of acting in support of Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps and Iraq's Kataib Hezbollah. US prosecutors said the same men, arrested on Tuesday, had played a role in nearly 20 attacks across the United States and Europe. Separately, Mohammad Mokhber, an adviser to Iran's supreme leader, Mojtaba Khamenei, warned Europe that any complicity in the US blockade would be "an act of war that will be etched in the nation's historical memory."

Bessent has led the sanctions campaign, known as Operation Economic Outcast, since August 2026, according to Reuters, Fox News and Voice of America. The campaign aims to pressure Tehran into ending attacks on shipping and reopening the Strait of Hormuz. The strait has been largely closed since the United States and Israel attacked Iran on February 28, 2026. The New York Times reported that at least six other US officials left their Washington-area residences last year for a military base or other protected housing. Among them were Stephen Miller, now the White House chief of staff, and Pam Bondi, then the attorney general.

Iran has denied in the past that it plotted attacks against officials or other people on US soil.
