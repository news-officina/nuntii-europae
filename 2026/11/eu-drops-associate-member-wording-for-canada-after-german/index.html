EU drops 'associate member' wording for Canada after German-led objection

The EU and Canada adopted a joint partnership declaration in Montreal covering trade, research and raw materials, but only after Berlin and several other capitals forced the contested term out of the text.

3 November 2026

Prime Minister Mark Carney nonetheless told the Montreal summit: "Today, we move towards associate membership for Canada, and we launch a new alliance for the future that will deliver now and in the future with like-minded nations." The statement, adopted in a written procedure on the evening of 28 October after days of talks, avoids the associate member term altogether. Prime Minister Mark Carney still told the summit: "Today, we move towards associate membership for Canada, and we launch a new alliance for the future that will deliver now and in the future with like-minded nations."

The partnership covers trade, critical raw materials, research, health, a new Youth Mobility Partnership and political ties, according to European Council President António Costa, who said the two sides chose a new course in Montreal. Costa will host Carney at the European Council in June 2027. Before that, a meeting between the College of the European Commission and the Canadian government is set for the spring. It is to work out the details of the proposal.

In September 2026, Commission President Ursula von der Leyen first offered Canada 'associate membership' in her State of the Union speech in Strasbourg, according to EU diplomats without broadly consulting member states beforehand. The move surprised EU capitals. On 27 October Germany broke the silence procedure and opposed the term 'associate member'. According to EU diplomats, Germany led the opposition, called the term a red line and wanted it fully removed from the text. EU diplomats also cited the unclear meaning of the planned special status and insufficient prior coordination between the Commission and the Council as reasons for the resistance. According to EU diplomats, EU capitals were wary because the status's legal contours remain undefined.

The resistance had built in the 48 hours before the summit. At least four countries broke the silence procedure before the midday deadline on 27 October, Politico reported, with several refusing to sign a text containing the phrase. According to dpa reporting from Brussels diplomats, Chancellor Friedrich Merz and other EU heads of state ensured the terms were cut from the draft declaration, with Italy and France also objecting. Carney brushed the loss aside: "The good news is it doesn't matter about specific names or nomenclature. What matters is the difference that it will make to the lives of Canadians."

Carney said Canada and the EU aim to conclude negotiations on associate membership by June 2027. Carney said the full substance and scope of the new form of membership would be determined in the coming months. Washington has already stated its position. US President Donald Trump called the proposal ridiculous. According to the CBC, he warned he might treat it as a "hostile act" and respond with high tariffs or by ending trade with Europe "on many things".
