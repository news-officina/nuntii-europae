Ten northern European nations to sign pact to pool their naval forces against Russia

The agreement unites the countries of the Joint Expeditionary Force around the Royal Navy's plan to align doctrine, training and equipment across their fleets. The commander of the Royal Navy says the force will complement NATO.

9 November 2026, updated 13 November 2026

Correction: An earlier version said the agreement had been signed. The piece was written on 9 November, before the planned signing on 10 November.

The ten Joint Expeditionary Force countries were due to sign an agreement on 10 November to partner their naval forces against future threats from Russia. Denmark, Estonia, Finland, Iceland, Latvia, Lithuania, the Netherlands, Norway, Sweden and the United Kingdom set out the commitment in July, in the Royal Navy's Maritime Proposition: to align doctrine, training, planning, sustainment and capability development so that equipment, parts, ammunition and personnel can be swapped or mixed across their fleets. First Sea Lord General Gwyn Jenkins, the Royal Navy's most senior officer, said the forces, commanded from Northwood, the Royal Navy's main maritime headquarters in London, will complement NATO and respond faster to crises.

Norway's Prime Minister, Jonas Gahr Støre, called the initiative a direct response to American calls for Europe and Canada to take more responsibility for Allied territory's security.

The initiative follows a shift in American policy. In July 2026, El País reported that Washington had told European allies it would cut the fighter aircraft it offers NATO in a crisis by a third. It would also halve its strategic bombers and no longer provide submarines. Alliance sources described the plan as burden sharing, and as one step toward a more European NATO. European allies and Canada have since moved to fill the gaps in the alliance's security umbrella.

All ten Joint Expeditionary Force nations formally agreed to the Maritime Proposition on 9 July 2026. Norway's Minister of Defence, Tore O. Sandvik, said the initiatives show that Europe and Canada are taking greater responsibility for maritime security and promoting more equitable burden-sharing in NATO. The result, he said, will be a stronger Europe and a stronger NATO.

Next week, HMS Prince of Wales will host the Atlantic Future Forum and the International Sea Power Conference in Oslo. Jenkins will lead the Royal Navy's involvement, with European military and political leaders attending. Iceland, meanwhile, is to host the next Joint Expeditionary Force leaders' summit in Reykjavik in spring 2027, as the force's defence ministers confirmed in October.
