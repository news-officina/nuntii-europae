EU leaders gather in Dublin to debate who speaks for Europe in Ukraine talks

The debate, called at Italy's request, will not produce a name before December. Poland objects to western Europe negotiating over the east, while Estonia says the existing envoys are enough.

12 November 2026

EU leaders meet in Dublin for an informal summit to debate who should speak for Europe in the Ukraine talks, at the request of Italian Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni. An EU source said the summit will only open the debate. Any candidate will be named in December. Estonian Prime Minister Kristen Michal says Europe already has its envoys in European Council President António Costa and Kaja Kallas, the EU's foreign policy chief.

Poland has already objected to the idea that western Europe should speak for the whole continent. Last week, Polish foreign minister Radek Sikorski criticised the Shell Bay format, in which delegates from France, Germany and the UK represented Europe. He said: "The last thing we want is western Europe dealing with Russia over the heads of the east Europeans." Some European capitals oppose Kaja Kallas for the role, arguing that her strong criticism of Russia and President Vladimir Putin makes her unsuited to the negotiations.

Estonia has rejected calls for a new arrangement, with Michal saying the two people for the job had already been agreed on. Their mandate comes directly from the Council, he said, so there is no need for a separate plan to be agreed among member states first. "Both are clearly accountable to EU countries," he added. As for Putin's preferences, Michal was blunt: "If Russians don't like it, too bad," because Europe should be represented by people accountable to the EU, not by those Putin likes.

The question has divided member states and grown more urgent after two days of talks in Abu Dhabi on the war. There, representatives of Russia, the United States and Ukraine met face to face, while the European Union sat at a separate, wider table with only senior civil servants present. In response to Meloni's letter, Costa wrote that the interests of the European Union must be represented by its institutions, in accordance with the Treaties. Politico read the wording as a hint that he wants the job himself.

Ukrainian president Volodymyr Zelensky has urged the EU to complete the appointment by the end of November, so that all sides could "speak at the level of leaders". US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner want to speed up the negotiating process, leaving European governments and EU institutions limited time to agree on their representation.
