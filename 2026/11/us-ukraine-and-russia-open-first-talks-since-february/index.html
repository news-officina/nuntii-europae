US, Ukraine and Russia open first talks since February in Abu Dhabi

Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff mediate two days of talks on security guarantees and territory, while Russian officials keep European representatives out of the main negotiations.

10 November 2026

Tuesday's talks in Abu Dhabi, mediated by Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff, two officials of the United States government, will exclude senior European officials from the main negotiations due to Russian objections. Washington is pushing for Europe to join future rounds. Senior European officials, including security advisers from the UK, Germany and France and representatives of NATO, the western defensive alliance, and institutions of the European Union, are present, but Russia has kept them out of the main negotiations.

The three parties see the place of Europe in the process differently. Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said on Monday 9 November that the talks were between Russia and Ukraine, with mediation by the United States. Kushner and Witkoff have proposed bringing Europeans into the discussion after the trilateral, and want this week's meeting to be the last round without them. The French presidency said there could be no durable settlement in Ukraine without taking European security into account, and no agreement on security in Europe without the Europeans.

The talks follow a set agenda over two days, according to reporting by Politico and the Kyiv Post. The first day covers security guarantees for Ukraine and the possible reshaping of Europe's security architecture. The second turns to territory and other questions. A senior US official said the territorial issues cannot be settled first. "They will not solve the Donetsk issue and the territorial issue until they know what the security architecture is going to be after the war," the official said.

Moscow's demands cover every region it claims. Russia demands that Ukraine recognise Russian control over Crimea and the four regions it claims to have illegally annexed, Donetsk, Luhansk, Zaporizhzhia and Kherson. It also demands that Ukraine withdraw its forces from all four, including areas Russia has never occupied. Ukraine has rejected these demands outright. Reuters reported on 10 November, citing a US official who briefed reporters before the talks, that both sides have nevertheless shown private willingness to consider a territorial exchange. The official called it "a little bit of flexibility."

The question of who speaks for Europe now moves to Dublin. EU leaders meet there later this week for an informal summit of the European Union in Dublin, Ireland, where they will discuss representation in the Ukraine peace talks, according to Politico. In order to end the war, you need to have Europe at the table, a senior US official said, according to Euronews.
