Six reported dead and 16 wounded after man opens fire on Krakow's Main Market Square

Police overpowered the 24-year-old after a gun fight, and prosecutors say the same investigation covers three killings in his home town. Karol Nawrocki has declared two days of national mourning.

9 November 2026

A masked 24-year-old drove onto Krakow's Main Market Square on Saturday afternoon, opened fire on pedestrians and, after a crash at the crossing with Dietla street, fired at police, who returned fire and overpowered him, Polish Radio 24 and Kielce TVP reported. Six people died and sixteen were wounded, including two police officers, a toll the two outlets dispute in part. President Karol Nawrocki declared two days of national mourning for 8 and 9 November.

The National Prosecutor's Office said the investigation covers the attempted killing of many others. In Brussels, European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen said Europe joins Poland in pain and solidarity after the attack. The European Parliament lowered the flags of Poland and the European Union in front of its seat in solidarity, the Romanian news site Gandul reported. Von der Leyen called the attack in Krakow tragic.

Interior Minister Marcin Kierwiński said preliminary findings point to a Polish national from Ostrowiec Świętokrzyskie who used an automatic rifle and acted alone. "Everything indicates he was fascinated by far-right ideologies," he said. The attacker is in hospital in a serious condition, according to RMF FM.

National Prosecutor's Office spokesman Przemysław Nowak said the evidence indicates the same person stands behind both the Krakow attack and the killings in Ostrowiec Świętokrzyski. Police found the bodies in the flat of Michał O., the 24-year-old detained after the Krakow attack, according to the regional news site Kielce TVP and RMF FM. Police said the dead were a 53-year-old man and two women aged 52 and 21. Deputy inspector Andrzej Fijołek of the Police National Headquarters said the dead were the parents and sister of the detained man.

Counter-terrorist officers and technicians worked all night at Michał O.'s flat with a prosecutor taking part, RMF FM and Kielce TVP reported. Police entered it by breaking a balcony window with a lift and flying a drone in, and the bodies were secured for detailed examination. Prime Minister Donald Tusk called senior constables Jakub Kulik and Artur Moranski "true heroes". Officials said one officer has been brought out of a coma, while Kulik is fighting for his life with one lung not functioning.
