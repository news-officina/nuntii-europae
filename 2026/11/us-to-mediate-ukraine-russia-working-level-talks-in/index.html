US to mediate Ukraine-Russia working-level talks in Abu Dhabi on 10 and 11 November

Russia will send a delegation led by presidential aide Vladimir Medinsky, according to Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov, while European governments are unlikely to be at the table. The European Commission will host separate talks on Ukraine's post-war recovery in Brussels on 4 and 5 November.

3 November 2026

The United States will mediate working-level talks between Ukraine and Russia in Abu Dhabi on 10 and 11 November, announced Steve Witkoff, the US special envoy. Discussions will focus on post-war security arrangements, de-escalation, humanitarian efforts and next steps toward peace, Witkoff said. The European Commission will host separate talks on 4 and 5 November on Ukraine's post-war recovery.

Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said Russia will send a delegation to Abu Dhabi led by Vladimir Medinsky, a presidential aide, with officials from several agencies, including the foreign ministry. The president must still approve the delegation's exact composition, Peskov said. European representatives cannot take part in the talks, he said. The meetings are an agreed trilateral format, Peskov said, bringing together Russia and Ukraine with the United States as mediator.

President Volodymyr Zelensky said during a visit to Berlin on 3 November that the Abu Dhabi talks would focus above all on security guarantees for Ukraine and on its post-war reconstruction plan. He wants the most sensitive issues, including territorial ones, to be discussed at leaders' level. Zelensky said he hopes the talks will lead to a three-way meeting of leaders including President Vladimir Putin. "We are counting on the fact that work in this format and our other diplomatic steps will open the way to a trilateral format at the level of leaders," Zelensky said. Ukraine insists on Europe's participation in the negotiation process, Zelensky said, although European governments are unlikely to be at the table in Abu Dhabi.

The meeting hosted by the European Commission in Brussels is a continuation of October talks in Miami, Florida, and Oslo with largely the same participants, officials said. At working level, participants will take a detailed look at the proposals developed on post-war recovery, economic development and Ukraine's longer-term needs. Zelensky said Ukraine will need an additional 70 billion euros from European countries next year.

Chancellor Friedrich Merz, Germany's head of government, received Zelensky in Berlin on 3 November in a meeting not announced in advance, for security reasons. After the talks, Merz said he does not yet see Russia seriously disposed to negotiate peace. "Important that Russia finally and seriously engages in negotiations about a ceasefire and a subsequent peace agreement," he said. Germany would continue supporting Ukraine until the end of the war, he said, and negotiations must lead to a ceasefire and reconciliation.
