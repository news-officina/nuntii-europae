One Polish court now has two managements and neither has given way

A weekend order replaced the Constitutional Tribunal's leadership, but only two of the six judges elected by the Sejm have taken their oaths. The dispute over who controls the court will turn on those oaths and on whether any EU move goes beyond statements.

7 November 2026

Judge Anna Korwin-Piotrowska, the Constitutional Tribunal judge with longest service, cited that experience in the judiciary when she ordered an immediate one-month suspension of Tribunal president Bogdan Święczkowski and vice-president Bartłomiej Sochański. In the same move, she announced she had assumed the top position of the court. Politico and the Polish legal portal Prawo.pl reported her order.

The question now is what will seat the remaining four judges elected by the Sejm in March. Both sides already hold a real lever. President Karol Nawrocki has accepted the oaths of only two of the six, Magdalena Bentkowska and Dariusz Szostak, which means the other four cannot take office through him alone. On Friday night he forced his way into the Tribunal building past a police cordon and remained inside for hours, past 4 a.m. Which one gives way will decide the matter.

Order against the tribunal president

Judge Korwin-Piotrowska issued an order on 13 March 2026 excluding Tribunal president Święczkowski from adjudication, the Polish legal news site prawo.pl reported. The order also cited his non-compliance with an interim measure issued by the European Court of Human Rights on 5 May 2026. That measure, quoted by a European Commission spokesperson, required Polish authorities to "refrain from hindering the taking up and exercise of judicial duties by judges appointed by the Sejm in March 2026".

During the night President Nawrocki called for the coercive forces that the Polish government had directed to the Constitutional Tribunal to leave its premises, and for the restoration of what he called the legal order. He described the events as a "forcible takeover of a constitutional institution". He stayed in the building until around 5 a.m. Before leaving, he convened urgent consultations with representatives of the parliamentary forces for 31 October, to stabilize the political situation and restore constitutional order.

Venice Commission opinion

At its 148th Plenary Session in Venice on 10 October 2026, the Venice Commission, the Council of Europe's advisory body on constitutional law, adopted its opinion on the Polish President's powers in the oath-taking of Constitutional Tribunal judges. The presidency answered three days later. President Karol Nawrocki's spokesperson said the opinion has no legal force and that the Commission has purely advisory competence. Speaking in Polish, the spokesperson said it would not influence the president's decisions.

On 31 October, Hungary's Viktor Orbán backed Nawrocki on X, writing: "I never thought @donaldtusk would sink to such depths as to stage a coup d'état against his own people. I sincerely hope President @NawrockiKn succeeds in his efforts to uphold Poland's constitutional order." A day earlier, United States ambassador Thomas Rose told Polish media that the events appear to be an unprecedented confrontation over the government's use of state power and the independence of a constitutional institution. America stands on Poland's side without judging its internal affairs, Rose said, and he urged all parties to calm down and avoid escalation, stressing that Poland's strength must come from within.

Nawrocki's declaration

The president's own statement went further. Nawrocki's 31 October declaration, quoted in Polish by Prawo.pl, said that "there was a breach of public order in the Republic of Poland and a forcible takeover of a constitutional body". He cut short a trip to Senegal to deal with the matter. During the night he also held consultations with Zbigniew Kapiński, the First President of the Supreme Court, whom he described as likewise deeming the actions of those who seized the Tribunal unlawful.

Taken together, the positions describe one court with two managements. The Venice Commission's opinion of 13 October holds that the five judges who swore without the president are already full judges, and the oath can be completed without him if he refuses. The European Commission has said appointments lie with the Sejm and that the Tribunal must function under the Polish constitution and Court of Justice case law, and is closely monitoring the crisis. President Nawrocki, for his part, calls the weekend's events a forcible seizure of a constitutional organ.

For the reader, the dispute comes down to two offices that both act as if they own the court. The presidency's side is not only words. On 19 October 2026, President Karol Nawrocki swore in newly appointed judges and one assessor before him at a ceremony, and congratulated them. He said it commands his greatest respect that they chose to serve the national community, and called their decision very important for building the foundations of Poland's constitutional order.

The two offices that will shape those steps met once already. Szymon Hołownia, the Marshal of the Sejm, Poland's lower house of parliament, sat down with President Karol Nawrocki on 31 October, according to tvp.info. The subject was the Constitutional Tribunal and possible ways out of the growing crisis around it.
