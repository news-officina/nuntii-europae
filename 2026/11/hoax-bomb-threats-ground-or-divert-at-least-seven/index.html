Hoax bomb threats ground or divert at least seven planes across Europe

Three of the affected flights were British, and one was escorted by the Italian Air Force. All alerts were later confirmed to be false alarms.

4 November 2026

A wave of hoax bomb threats grounded or diverted at least seven planes across Europe on 3 November, three of them British flights. Airlines affected included LOT of Poland, Scandinavian Airlines and Wizz Air, and one flight was escorted by the Italian Air Force. The threats prompted searches and diversions before all alerts were confirmed to be false alarms.

The wave began a day earlier, with a Wizz Air diversion to Budapest on 2 November. The flight, from London Luton to Sofia, had received a bomb threat while it was in the air. The British government blamed the disruption to its flights on a malicious hoax, using that phrase in its public response. It gave no details on who was behind the threats or how they were carried out.

One flight, though, landed nowhere near its destination. A LOT Polish Airlines service from London Heathrow to Warsaw received a bomb alarm in the air and was diverted to Warsaw-Modlin Airport, landing at 10:01 on 3 November, according to the airport's website. In Rome, two Ryanair planes, one arriving from Lisbon and one from Dublin, were searched. The Dublin flight was escorted by the Italian air force until it landed.

Bulgarian officials said the threats arrived by email, according to Lyubomir Nikolov, secretary general at the country's Interior Ministry. He said the senders had begun targeting certain airlines, forcing aircraft to change course, and that efforts to identify them were continuing. The incidents also drew a response from the European Commission. Christophe Hansen, the EU's food and agriculture commissioner, said the bloc was taking them very seriously, calling them a deliberate attempt to disrupt the food supply chain and undermine consumer confidence.

In Ireland, 13 SuperValu stores were affected by what Musgrave, the Irish wholesaler that owns the SuperValu chain, described as a cyber incident, with replenishment of chilled and frozen ranges under way as of Monday. Hackers attacked at least one supermarket in Estonia and raised the temperature in its refrigeration units to 50C, according to public broadcaster ERR. Commission spokesman Thomas Regnier declined to attribute blame but said the incidents show how cyber-attacks can disrupt essential services and affect citizens' daily lives, and that they are not isolated.
