Russia veto leaves UN mission in Western Sahara without a mandate

The Security Council failed to renew MINURSO by 31 October after Russia blocked an American draft, and Moscow's own counter-draft fell short of the nine votes needed. France warned that an abrupt closure would be exceedingly alarming.

31 October 2026

Russia vetoed a United States draft resolution in the Security Council on 30 October, leaving the United Nations mission in Western Sahara without a renewed mandate. The draft received 11 votes in favour and three abstentions. For the first time since MINURSO was created, its mandate was left to expire on 31 October, an outcome several outlets reported as ending the mission.

The American text would have extended MINURSO until 31 October 2027. It would also have removed the mission's role in organising a referendum on Western Sahara's future, and asked the Secretary-General for proposals on its structure by 30 January 2027. The United Kingdom's delegate expressed immense disappointment at Russia's veto, saying it jeopardizes the mission's deployment. France's delegate warned that an abrupt closure of MINURSO would be exceedingly alarming, given resumed tensions in the territory.

Russia responded on the same day with its own draft resolution, S/2026/870, which proposed a technical one-year extension of MINURSO without changing its name or mandate. Moscow called on what it described as reasonable members to support the text. Vassily Nebenzia, Russia's ambassador to the United Nations, called his veto a veto of salvation and described his draft as the last chance to keep the mission in its original form. He said opponents would bear responsibility for the mission's disappearance.

Russia's counter-draft failed to reach the nine votes required for adoption, drawing three votes in favour, one against from the United States and eleven abstentions. Because the American negative vote fell below the threshold for a veto, the United States suffered no such consequence. China's delegate said it abstained because consultations on the American proposal were insufficient, adding that the mission's mandate should not be used to exert political pressure. The United States said it stood ready to work with the Secretary-General on an orderly drawdown that protects UN personnel and addresses security and humanitarian needs.

Algeria reacted on 31 October with what it called profound regret. The Algerian government blamed the outcome on what it described as excessive bias in the American draft in favour of Morocco. Removing the letter R, the reference to the referendum on self-determination, emptied that right of its substance, Algeria said. Making autonomy the sole basis of negotiations had the same effect, in its view.
